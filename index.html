The process

Five steps. You will always know where we are.

Each stage ends with something you can see, and nothing moves forward without your word.

01

Strategy

A conversation about your practice — who you serve, what makes your approach yours, and what this website genuinely needs to do. Design decisions come later, and they come from here.

02

Design

A visual direction drawn from your work rather than from a trend. You see it, you respond to it, we refine it together.

03

Development

Built properly — quick to load, clear to navigate, and considered on a phone as carefully as on a desktop. Contacting you is never more than a tap away.

Page titles, descriptions and proper indexing with Google, so the people already searching your name find the right thing. Honest groundwork, no inflated promises.

A therapist in private practice

She held more than one domain name, with a single simple site between them — serviceable, but built to hold information rather than to represent her. The first decision was which to build properly. We began with her personal practice, then followed it with a second, educational site. Both were handed over completely.

What we built

  • A custom homepage of clearly separated sections, each editable on its own
  • A writing archive she publishes to directly, without involving us
  • An announcements area, with pinning and dated items that retire on their own
  • Navigation, footer and contact details under her control
  • Contact form and custom domain, configured and connected

What she was handed

  • A written handover guide covering every routine task, in plain language
  • A first-three-steps checklist, so nothing was ambiguous on day one
  • Image guidance so the site stays quick as it grows
  • A troubleshooting table for the small things that come up
  • Full ownership of her content, editable at any hour without permission or invoice
The measure of this project is not the launch. It is that she has not needed to contact us to change a word since.

The harder half is listening.

Who you would be working with

Fernkeep is one person, on purpose.

I started building websites by helping a family member open a restaurant. I got the technical side right on the first attempt and the site was still wrong — because I had built what I thought a restaurant website should be, rather than what theirs was.

The lesson has shaped everything since. Execution is the easy half. The harder and more valuable half is listening closely enough to understand what someone has actually built, then finding the visual language that belongs to it rather than to a trend.

You will speak to me at every stage. Nobody is handed to an account manager and nothing is subcontracted — which limits how many projects run at once, and is the reason the work stays careful.
